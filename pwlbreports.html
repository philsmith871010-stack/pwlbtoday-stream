Housing authorities, and councils without an HRA

One fund or two, and the report knows the difference.

The 2026 proposals treat a housing authority differently at almost every indicator, and differently again by how it apportions debt. Here it all follows from one answer on the first screen.

01

No HRA

Eleven rows, and housing is never mentioned. No second fund, no pooling question, no controls you cannot use.

02

One pool

The HRA is asked for seven rows, not eleven — no loans of its own, no investments, no liquidity allowance.

03

Two pools

LB1, LB2 and LB3 per fund, and LB6 between them. Inter-fund lending capped at the surplus the lending fund actually has.

04

Measured on its own income

Affordability for the General Fund, the HRA and the whole authority — the HRA's struck against rents.

What it does not claim

A drafting aid, not a compliance statement.

It produces the indicators the Code requires. It does not certify that your authority complies with anything.

  • Nothing is extrapolated. Every year is a figure you entered.
  • The loan book is a starting point. It cannot see market debt or borrowing you intend.
  • Two pools are not split for you. Nothing published says which pool a loan sits in, so it asks rather than guesses.
  • Two requirements are yours alone — and are named, not dropped.
  • Check every figure before committee. It is a draft, and says so on itself.
